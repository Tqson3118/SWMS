# 📖 Đọc 3 — Why Losing Hurts Twice as Much (~340 từ)

> Chủ đề: **loss aversion** — nỗi đau của "mất" mạnh gấp đôi niềm vui của "được". Chú ý các câu Present Perfect + Past Simple xen kẽ.

---

Here is a coin game. Heads, you win 200,000 dong. Tails, you lose 200,000. Do you take it?

Almost nobody takes it — and if you keep raising the win side, people only accept when it reaches roughly **twice** the loss. Psychologists **have replicated** this for forty years, on every continent, with money, candies, and points in video games. Losing hurts about twice as much as winning pleases. Kahneman's summary **has entered** the textbooks: *losses loom larger than gains.*

Once you see it, you find it everywhere. Investors **have held** losing stocks for years, because selling would turn a paper loss into a real one — and the mind treats "real loss" like a small death. She **kept** the job that made her sick, because leaving meant "losing" the title, and titles feel like limbs. I **have done** both myself, more than once, and the memory still stings.

Companies **have understood** this about you for a long time. "Don't miss out" works better than "gain this". Trials that end — "your access expires Friday" — hurt more than bonuses that begin. Insurance has built an industry on the fear of losing what you **have not even lost yet**.

Is loss aversion always an enemy? No. It **has kept** families from gambling away food money; it makes us guard what matters. The trouble starts when the guarding has no limit — when everything must be kept, forever, at any cost.

So the next time you refuse something new, ask the simple question this month **has taught** you: am I choosing what I want — or am I only running from what I might lose?
