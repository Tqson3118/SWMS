## Bài 1 — The Four Instruments (~440 từ)

Phase 2 began, four months ago, with a single sentence type: *I was sitting on the doorstep when suddenly...* You are now holding four instruments, and this last reading is simply the inventory.

**Month one gave you the past.** Past Continuous painted the background; Past Simple struck the events; *used to* carried the long shapes of a life. With those you learned the most human of all skills: telling a story so that another mind can stand inside it. Memory, childhood, the narratives we build about ourselves — the psychology of who we were.

**Month two gave you the ledger.** Present Perfect counted experience to the present moment: *I have tried, I have never regretted, I have been learning since March*. You learned to weigh decisions, to name the biases that bend judgment — anchors, sunk costs, the theater of certainty — and to speak about your own life as something still open, still accumulating, still yours to audit.

**Month three gave you the crowd.** Past Perfect layered pasts beneath pasts; conditionals built the grammar of what is always true, what may come, and what exists only in the mirror of *if*. Asch, Milgram, Cialdini: you learned how groups bend eyes, how authority borrows hands, how persuasion presses six ancient buttons — and how one honest voice, one pointing finger, one small pause breaks the spell. You can now argue gently with a room.

**Month four gave you the machine.** Passive voice handed you the scientist's grammar: *habits are built, rewards are delivered, you are designed by your environment* — and the humility of the marshmallow children taught you the rest. You learned that willpower is smaller than architecture, that identity is built from votes, that plateaus are the signature of compounding, and that the person who wins is rarely the strongest — merely the one whose room is arranged for the good loop.

Put the four together and notice what they actually are: one instrument for time, one for experience, one for possibility, one for process. Every story worth telling, every argument worth making, every plan worth executing uses at least two of them. The B1 threshold you have been walking toward is not a list of tenses — it is exactly this: a person who can move between the four instruments without translating in their head, choosing the right one because the meaning demands it.

Look also at what the four months did to your *content*, not just your grammar. You have read about memory's storytelling, loss aversion, Asch's lines, bystanders, the dopamine loop, the marshmallow, the vote-casting self. That is not vocabulary; that is a small liberal education. When Phase 3 asks you to write essays about personality and communication, it will not be building on sand.

The final week is a ritual, not a test only. Re-read one favorite piece from each month. Listen to your earliest voice recording if you kept one — the difference is the plateau of the invisible, proving itself at last. Take the Phase 2 finale honestly, and then rest; you have earned a genuine rest, the kind that is the second half of the work.

Then, when you are ready, Phase 3 waits with a new instrument — the sentence inside the sentence: *the woman who taught me this has never met me.* You have been reading her for four months.

**Câu hỏi:**
1. Name the four instruments, one per month, with their grammar.
2. What, according to the article, is the B1 threshold actually?
3. What is suggested as the final-week ritual?
