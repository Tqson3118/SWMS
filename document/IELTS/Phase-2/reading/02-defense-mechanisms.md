# 📖 Đọc 2 — Defense Mechanisms (~400 từ)

> Freud đơn giản hóa — và tự nhận diện bạn đang dùng cơ chế nào.

---

Your mind is like a castle. Sometimes reality attacks — pain, failure, shame — and the castle has walls. Those walls are called **defense mechanisms**. Sigmund Freud's daughter, Anna Freud, mapped them. Here are the five most famous.

**Denial**: refusing to see what is true. The doctor says the news is bad; the patient hears nothing. A man's marriage is ending; he plans next year's anniversary trip. Denial is not lying — it is the mind's anesthetic. It helps for a while, but anesthesia is not medicine.

**Repression**: the mind buries the memory so deep that you cannot find it. A woman remembers nothing before age seven — a whole childhood, gone. Repression is different from suppression: suppression is choosing to push a feeling away ("I'll think about this later"); repression is the mind doing it for you, without permission.

**Projection**: your feeling is too painful to own, so you put it on someone else. If you secretly dislike a coworker, you may become convinced that HE dislikes YOU. You are not seeing him; you are seeing your own reflection wearing his face.

**Rationalization**: building a reasonable explanation for an unreasonable choice. "I didn't fail the interview — I decided that company wasn't right for me anyway." The explanation sounds adult. The wound underneath is still a child's.

**Displacement**: the emotion is real, the target is wrong. Your boss shouts at you; you shout at your little brother; he kicks the dog. Emotion travels down the ladder until it finds someone who cannot fight back.

Are defenses bad? No — they are walls, and walls protect. A castle without walls does not stand. But a castle that is ALL walls has no doors — nothing new can enter, nothing can grow.

The mature question is not "How do I destroy my defenses?" It is: "Which wall do I open a door in today?"
