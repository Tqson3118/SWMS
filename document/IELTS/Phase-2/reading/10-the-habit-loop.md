# 📖 Đọc 1 — The Habit Loop (~400 từ)

> Bài mở màn tháng 4 — gạch chân mỗi câu Passive (be + V3) bạn tìm thấy.

---

In the 1990s, a researcher named Ann Graybiel **was training** rats to run a maze. As the rats learned, something strange **was recorded** in their brains: at first, activity exploded everywhere — the maze was new, dangerous, interesting. But as the route **was learned**, the noise went quiet. By the end, the whole run **was handled** by one small burst at the start and one at the finish. The middle had gone silent.

That silence is what a habit is. Once the loop **is established**, the brain stops participating. This is why you can drive home and remember nothing about the drive — the route **was run** by machinery, not by you.

The machinery has four parts, and they **have been mapped** by a century of research. First, a **cue** — a signal that says "this situation again". Then a **craving** — the brain's prediction of the reward. Then a **response** — the behavior itself. And finally the **reward**, which **is delivered** and, in that instant, teaches the brain: *remember this loop, it worked.*

Notice what is NOT in that list: a decision. Habits **are not chosen**; they are compiled, the way a program is compiled, from hundreds of repetitions. The good news hides inside that fact. Because habits are mechanical, they **can be re-engineered** mechanically. The cue can be removed, the response can be replaced, the reward can be redirected to a better loop — and the craving, that stubborn middle child, follows the others around like a dog on a long leash.

This is the quiet revolution in behavior science. For most of the twentieth century, self-change **was framed** as a battle of character: willpower against desire, virtue against appetite. The research of the last thirty years **has redrawn** the picture. Willpower **is spent** in moments; design **is kept** for years. The person who wins is rarely the strongest — it is the one whose environment **has been arranged** so that the good loop is the easy loop.

Duke University researchers once **estimated** that about 40% of daily actions **are performed** on autopilot, not decided. You are, right now, mostly a bundle of loops wearing a name. That sounds like an insult until you see what it really means: nearly half of your life **is run** by a system that can be edited — not by heroism, but by small, patient engineering.

The rat, the maze, the silence. The habit loop **was found** in animals; it **has been confirmed** in you. Now it will be audited by the only creature in the maze who can read the map.

**Câu hỏi:**
1. What happened in the rats' brains as the maze was learned?
2. What are the four parts of the habit loop, and what is NOT in the list?
3. According to the article, how is self-change "redrawn" by modern research?

1. Activity went quiet in the middle of the run — the whole route was handled by bursts at start and finish; the brain stopped participating.
2. Cue, craving, response, reward; a decision is NOT in the list — habits are compiled, not chosen.
3. Not a battle of character/willpower but engineering: cues removed, responses replaced, environments arranged so the good loop is the easy loop.
