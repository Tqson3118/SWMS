# 📖 Đọc 3 — Six Buttons (~350 từ)

> Chủ đề: **Cialdini và 6 nguyên tắc thuyết phục** — 6 nút bấm mà quảng cáo, chính trị và kẻ lừa đảo dùng mỗi ngày. Chú ý câu điều kiện Zero ("If you..., ...") trong bài.

---

Robert Cialdini **spent** years studying what he first called the "compliance professionals": salespeople, fundraisers, cult recruiters, con artists. He **had** expected to find tricks. Instead he found six buttons, installed in every human being, that professionals simply press.

**One: reciprocity.** If someone gives you something — a gift, a favor, a free sample — you feel you owe them. Evolution built that debt-collector into us, and it works even when the gift is tiny and the favor was never requested. Hare Krishnas **handed** out flowers at airports for exactly this reason.

**Two: commitment and consistency.** If you said yes once — signed a petition, told a friend your opinion — you will say yes again, bigger, to stay consistent with the person you believe you are. Salespeople sell small first.

**Three: social proof.** If everyone is doing it, it must be right — or at least safe. Canned laughter makes jokes seem funnier; hotels tell you most guests reuse towels; a queue outside a restaurant **sells** the food better than the menu.

**Four: liking.** If you like someone — they're attractive, familiar, similar to you, or just say your name often — you comply more. Tupperware built an empire on friendship itself: the party host was never a stranger.

**Five: authority.** If a person in a uniform or with a title says it, your questions fade. Milgram **had** shown this in a basement at Yale; advertisers just put actors in white coats.

**Six: scarcity.** If it is rare, limited, or about to disappear, it becomes valuable instantly. "Only three left", "offer ends tonight" — the fear of losing **outvotes** the joy of gaining, every single time.

None of these buttons is evil; they are shortcuts that usually serve us well. The problem is that professionals **have** been pressing them professionally for a century. Your defense is not cynicism — it is simply noticing the finger on the button. When you feel a sudden debt, a rush of consistency, a fear of missing out — pause and ask: *who pressed that?*

**Câu hỏi:**
1. What are the six buttons? Name them.
2. Give the example given for social proof and for scarcity.
3. According to the article, what is the correct defense against these techniques?
