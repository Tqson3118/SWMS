# 📖 Đọc 1 — The Stories We Tell Ourselves (~330 từ)

> Bài mở màn Phase 2 — gạch chân mỗi câu Past Continuous bạn tìm thấy.

---

Psychologists have a beautiful idea: we are not just our memories — we are the **stories** we tell about our memories.

Two children grow up in the same house. One remembers a strict father who taught discipline. The other remembers a cold man who never smiled. Same house, same father — different stories. Which story is true? Both. Neither. The question itself is wrong.

Here is what matters: the story we choose changes who we become. A person who tells herself "I was abandoned" carries one life. A person who tells herself "I learned to be independent early" carries another. The facts are the same; the meaning is different.

Last year, I was talking with my grandmother about her childhood. She was eighty-one then, and her memories were fading — names, dates, faces. But the stories? The stories were sharp as glass. When she was twelve, she was walking to school when a stranger gave her an orange. That was during the war, when an orange was treasure. She kept the orange for three days before eating it.

"Why three days?" I asked.

"Because hope lasted longer than the orange," she said.

That is a story. Not the orange, not the stranger — the meaning she built around them. For seventy years, that story has been telling her: good things come, and they last if you hold them.

So ask yourself: which stories are you telling about your past? Are they stories of damage, or stories of survival? You cannot change what happened. But you are the author — not just the character — and the next chapter is unwritten.

## Câu hỏi

1. What did the two children remember differently about the same father?
2. Why did the grandmother keep the orange for three days?
3. According to the text, what can we change about our past?

---

**ĐÁP ÁN:**
1. One saw a strict father who taught discipline; the other saw a cold man who never smiled.
2. Because "hope lasted longer than the orange" — holding it felt like holding hope.
3. The story/meaning we build around it (we are the author, not just the character).
