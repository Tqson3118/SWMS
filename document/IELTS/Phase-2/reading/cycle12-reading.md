## Bài 1 — The Decider You Have Become (~430 từ)

You **have been learning** to decide for a month now, and you may not have noticed the moment the lessons started paying rent. So let me hold up the mirror.

A month ago, a price crossed out in red was a bargain. Now it is an anchor, and you **have been noticing** it everywhere — the "original" price, the first salary number, the "only 3 left". A month ago, your certainty felt like intelligence. Now you **have learned** to hear "I knew it all along" as a warning bell, not a compliment. A month ago, quitting meant weakness. Now you know to ask the only question that matters: *would I choose this again today?*

None of this **has made** you irrational-free. The biases **have not left** the building — they live in the walls. Kahneman, who **has studied** the mind for sixty years, said it plainly: knowing about biases does not switch them off. What knowing does is quieter and better: it gives you the *pause* — the half-second where you notice that your fast answer is fast, and ask it one question before signing anything.

You **have collected** the questions this month, like keys on a ring:
- *Who chose this frame?* (framing)
- *What would I see if I looked for the opposite?* (confirmation bias)
- *What have I already spent — and why is it voting?* (sunk cost)
- *Am I coming from a highlight reel?* (comparison, envy, FOMO)
- *Is this the 3 a.m. brain talking?* (fatigue, mood)
- *What does the boring number say?* (availability, vividness)
- *Has this slept overnight? Has it met a second opinion?* (pause, perspective)

One month **has not** turned you into a chess computer. But something **has changed** in the direction of your attention. Before, the world simply arrived: prices felt fair or unfair, people felt trustworthy or not, choices felt obvious or hard. Now, between the world and you, a question stands guard. That is the whole difference between a consumer of reality and a reader of it.

There is a last, gentle warning. The tools **have been sharp** this month — sharper than they look. Use them on prices, plans, purchases, and promises. But do not point them at everything forever; a person who runs a bias-check on every sandwich and every small kindness **has been missing** life while auditing it. The masters flip System 2 on for the expensive, irreversible rows of the spreadsheet — and let System 1 drive the rest, windows down, radio on.

That balance — care where it counts, ease everywhere else — is the real graduation certificate of this month. You **have earned** it. The next month widens the lens: from one deciding mind to many minds together — groups, crowds, and the strange things people **have been doing** to each other since forever, in the name of belonging.
