# 📖 Đọc 3 — The Marshmallow Test (~380 từ)

> Chủ đề: **self-control** — thí nghiệm bánh marshmallow và bài học thật 50 năm sau. Chú ý passive trong kể khoa học.

---

In the late 1960s, at a laboratory in Palo Alto, a four-year-old **was seated** at a table. One marshmallow **was placed** in front of her, and a deal **was offered**: eat it now, or wait alone for about fifteen minutes and get two. Then the researcher **left** the room, and a hidden camera **was turned** on.

What the camera recorded became one of the most famous fifteen minutes in psychology. Some children lasted seconds. Some **covered** their eyes, **sang** songs, **stroked** the marshmallow like a pet. A minority sat still and simply waited. Years later, follow-up studies **were run**: the children who had waited longer **were found** to have, on average, better school results and healthier habits as teenagers.

For a generation, the story **was told** simply: self-control **is measured** in one sitting; willpower is character; the future **is predicted** by a marshmallow. An entire industry of parenting advice and adult self-blame **was built** on that version.

Then the version **was tested** again — this time honestly. In 2018, a much larger study **was conducted** with better controls, and the famous correlation shrank dramatically. The children who waited came mostly from homes where promises **were kept**: two marshmallows **were actually delivered**, the second one showed up when the first one was promised. Children from less reliable homes **had learned**, rationally, to eat the first marshmallow now — because in their world, waiting **was not reliably rewarded**.

Read that again, because it is one of the kindest findings in the science of behavior. The "weak will" of a child was often just an accurate read of their environment. Delay of gratification **was never** a muscle in the soul; it is a bet on the future — and rational creatures bet only when the odds **have been demonstrated** to them.

The lesson for your habits is direct. You are not struggling because your willpower **was born** small. You are struggling because somewhere along the way, waiting **was not rewarded** — the diet that paid nothing, the study that led nowhere, the trust that **was broken** once too often. Self-control is not shouted into existence; it **is rebuilt** by keeping small promises to yourself, on schedule, until your own nervous system **is convinced** that the second marshmallow is real.

Then the waiting gets easier. Not because you became a saint — because you finally have evidence.
