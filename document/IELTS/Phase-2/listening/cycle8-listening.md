### Questions (source-dependent)

4. He uses a vacation and a colonoscopy to show how remembered evaluations can differ from lived experience.

### Questions (source-dependent)

1. Why does human decision-making frequently deviate from rational economic theory according to Sara Garofalo?
2. How does the concept of loss aversion explain human reactions to financial risks?
3. What are heuristics and why did humans evolve to use them?
4. How does the anchoring effect bias an individual's evaluation of numbers or prices?

<details>
<summary>Hidden answer key (open after attempting)</summary>

1. People rely on intuitive heuristics and emotional biases rather than calculating mathematical probabilities.
2. Losses are psychologically felt roughly twice as intensely as equivalent monetary gains.
3. They are mental shortcuts designed for rapid survival decisions, which can misfire in complex modern situations.
4. An initial piece of information sets a reference point that heavily distorts subsequent estimates.

</details>
