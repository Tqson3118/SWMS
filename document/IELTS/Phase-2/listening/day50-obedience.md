### Questions (source-dependent)

1. What shocking proportion of ordinary subjects complied fully in Stanley Milgram's obedience study?
2. How did the Stanford Prison Experiment demonstrate the psychological impact of institutional roles?
3. What is the primary difference between passive bystander behavior and active heroism?
4. How can individuals resist situational pressures to conform to unethical authority?

<details>
<summary>Hidden answer key (open after attempting)</summary>

1. Nearly two-thirds of participants delivered what they believed were maximum, lethal electrical shocks.
2. Assigned roles rapidly overwhelmed moral values, turning normal students into cruel guards or passive prisoners.
3. Bystanders remain silent accomplices, whereas heroes take active personal risks to oppose systemic injustice.
4. By cultivating situational awareness, questioning unjust authority, and celebrating everyday heroic action.

</details>

# 🎧 Listening Day 2 (Phase 2 — Month 3) — Obedience

> B1 listening: nghe về Milgram — obedience to authority.

## Listening Source
- **Primary:** TED: Philip Zimbardo — "The psychology of evil" — https://www.ted.com/talks/philip_zimbardo_on_the_psychology_of_evil
- **Duration:** 23:16
- **Transcript status:** Available
- **Date checked:** 2026-09-22
- **Level:** B1
- **Listening goal:** Nghe phân tích về thí nghiệm Milgram và tâm lý vâng lời quyền lực (Obedience to authority)
- **Fallback keyword:** TED-Ed the Milgram experiment obedience authority

## 2. Shadowing (15-20 phút) — 5 câu
1. If I were the subject, I would refuse — I think.
2. If you put people under authority, they often obey.
3. If the authority insists, most people will continue.
4. What would you do if you saw someone collapse?
5. If one person resists, others will follow.

Cách làm: nghe 1 câu → tạm dừng → nói lại → lặp 3 lần/câu.
