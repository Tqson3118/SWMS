### Questions (source-dependent)

1. How does experiencing failure trick our minds into underestimating our true capabilities?
2. What harmful reaction do people commonly have toward their self-esteem after social rejection?
3. What two-minute cognitive distraction technique does Winch recommend to stop negative rumination?
4. How can adopting self-compassion change our internal dialogue after making a mistake?

<details>
<summary>Hidden answer key (open after attempting)</summary>

1. Failure induces perceived helplessness, convincing people that success is impossible even when achievable.
2. People often begin berating themselves, damaging their own self-esteem when it is already hurting.
3. Engaging in a brief mentally demanding activity that redirects focus and disrupts depressive loops.
4. Treating oneself with the same warmth, patience, and encouragement offered to a dear friend.

</details>
