### Questions (source-dependent)

1. According to philosopher Ruth Chang, why are hard choices hard?
2. What does it mean for two alternatives to be 'on a par'?
3. How does Chang describe a 'drifter' who avoids making genuine hard choices?
4. What positive opportunity do hard choices offer for defining personal identity?

<details>
<summary>Hidden answer key (open after attempting)</summary>

1. They are hard because neither alternative is simply better, worse, or equal; the options are on a par.
2. They belong to the same league of value and are comparable, yet qualitatively different in kind.
3. Someone who lets fear, external rewards, or the path of least resistance dictate their life instead of creating reasons.
4. They allow individuals to exercise normative power and actively choose the kind of person they want to become.

</details>

# 🎧 Listening Day 3 (Phase 2 — Month 2) — Decision Stories

> B1 listening: nghe người thật kể một quyết định của đời họ — dạng "story of a choice".

## Listening Source
- **Primary:** TED: Ruth Chang — "How to make hard choices" — https://www.ted.com/talks/ruth_chang_how_to_make_hard_choices
- **Duration:** 14:41
- **Transcript status:** Available
- **Date checked:** 2026-09-22
- **Level:** B1
- **Listening goal:** Nghe các câu chuyện ra quyết định bước ngoặt và cấu trúc câu hỏi kinh nghiệm (Have you ever...?)
- **Fallback keyword:** TED Ruth Chang how to make hard choices

## 2. Shadowing (15-20 phút) — 5 câu
1. Have you ever made a snap decision?
2. I have never regretted that choice.
3. How many times have you changed your major?
4. She has been to Japan twice — both for work.
5. We have just finished the pros-and-cons list.

Cách làm: nghe 1 câu → tạm dừng → nói lại → lặp 3 lần/câu.
