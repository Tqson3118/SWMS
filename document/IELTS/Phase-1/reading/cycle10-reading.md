## Bài 1 — Love Is Not Countable (110 từ)

You cannot count love. You cannot say: "I have three loves" — well, you can, but it sounds strange. Love is a substance, like water or air: you have some, or a little, or a lot.

But you CAN count the things love makes: a kiss, two kisses... a promise, a hundred promises... a memory, a thousand memories.

Psychologists say happy couples collect small moments — not big ones. They don't need much passion every day. They need a little kindness every morning, a few words of thanks, some small surprises.

How much love do you need? Wrong question. How many small acts of love did you give today? That is the right question.

**Câu hỏi:**
1. Can you count love? What CAN you count?
2. Do happy couples need much passion every day, according to the text?
3. What is "the right question" at the end?

<details><summary>ĐÁP ÁN (nhấn để mở)</summary>

1. No — love is uncountable (like water/air). You can count what love makes: kisses, promises, memories.
2. No — they need a little kindness, a few words, some small surprises.
3. "How many small acts of love did you give today?"

</details>

## Bài 2 — Some Trust, Any Questions (110 từ)

Anna and Binh started couples therapy last month. The therapist asked them a simple question: "How much trust is left in this relationship?"

Anna thought for a while. "Some," she said. "Not much. But some."

"Good," said the therapist. "We don't need much. We need some — and we will grow it."

She gave them homework: every evening, tell each other three honest sentences. Not compliments — honest sentences. "I felt lonely today." "I need some quiet." "Thank you for the coffee."

After a month, Binh said: "I don't have any questions about us anymore. I have some hope."

Small honest words built some trust — and some was enough to start.

**Câu hỏi:**
1. How much trust was left at the start?
2. What was the homework?
3. What changed after a month?

<details><summary>ĐÁP ÁN (nhấn để mở)</summary>

1. Some — not much, but some.
2. Every evening, tell each other three honest sentences (not compliments).
3. Binh didn't have any questions anymore — he had some hope (trust grew).

</details>

## Bài 3 — A Few Dates, A Little Patience (100 từ)

Modern love is fast: apps show you a hundred faces in a minute. Swipe left, swipe right. A few messages, a few photos, then — a date or nothing.

But love itself is still slow. Real intimacy needs a few months of conversations, a little boredom survived together, a few small fights repaired.

Our grandparents knew this. They didn't have many choices, so they gave their one choice much patience.

Maybe the recipe is old but still true: fewer options, more attention. A few good dates beat a hundred swipes. A little patience builds a lot of love.

**Câu hỏi:**
1. Is modern love fast or slow?
2. What does real intimacy need?
3. "A few good dates beat ___ swipes" — điền.

<details><summary>ĐÁP ÁN (nhấn để mở)</summary>

1. Modern love (the apps) is fast, but love itself is still slow.
2. A few months of conversations, a little boredom survived together, a few small fights repaired.
3. a hundred.

</details>
