## Bài 1 — A Partner, Not a Project (110 từ)

My aunt gave me the best advice about love. She said: "Find a partner, not a project."

A project is a person you want to change: "He will be better when he gets a job." "She will be calmer after the baby." You fall in love with a future person, not the real person.

A partner is someone who is already growing — slowly, like a tree. You don't build a partner; you choose one. Then you build a life together.

"But people change!" I said. "Yes," she smiled. "People change the way trees grow. A project is a tree you want to move. A partner is a tree you water."

**Câu hỏi:**
1. What is a "project" in love?
2. What is a "partner"?
3. Complete: "A project is a tree you want to ___. A partner is a tree you ___."

<details><summary>ĐÁP ÁN (nhấn để mở)</summary>

1. A person you want to change (you love a future person, not the real one).
2. Someone already growing slowly — you choose them, then build a life together.
3. move / water.

</details>

## Bài 2 — The Five Apologies (120 từ)

An old couple told me their secret: five apologies a week. Not five big ones — five small ones.

"Sorry, I wasn't listening." "Sorry, the coffee is cold." "Sorry, I took the last piece."

The wife explained: "We stopped keeping score years ago. In the beginning, I counted: he said sorry twice, I said it four times — not fair! Then I understood: an apology is not a debt. It is a gift. You don't count gifts."

The husband added: "And sorry has two parts. First: I see the small hurt. Second: I will try again. A sorry without the second part is just a signature."

**Câu hỏi:**
1. How many apologies a week? Big or small?
2. Why did the wife stop counting?
3. What are the two parts of "sorry"?

<details><summary>ĐÁP ÁN (nhấn để mở)</summary>

1. Five small ones a week.
2. Because an apology is not a debt — it is a gift (you don't count gifts).
3. 1) I see the small hurt; 2) I will try again.

</details>

## Bài 3 — Love Languages (simplified, 130 từ)

Dr. Gary Chapman wrote a famous book about the five "love languages" — five ways people give and receive love.

**Words**: "You look great today." **Time**: an evening without phones. **Gifts**: a small thing that says "I thought of you." **Service**: fixing her bike, cooking his dinner. **Touch**: a hand on the shoulder.

Here is the interesting part: we usually give love in OUR language, but the other person receives love in THEIRS. You give words; she needs touch. You give gifts; he needs time. Two people love each other — and both feel unloved.

The lesson: learn your partner's language. Love in the language they hear.

**Câu hỏi:**
1. Name the five love languages.
2. Why can two people love each other and both feel unloved?
3. What is the lesson?

<details><summary>ĐÁP ÁN (nhấn để mở)</summary>

1. Words, Time, Gifts, Service, Touch.
2. Because they give love in their own language, not their partner's.
3. Learn your partner's language — love in the language they hear.

</details>
