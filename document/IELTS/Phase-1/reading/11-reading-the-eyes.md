# Reading 11 — Reading the Eyes (revised)

> Eyes can provide context, but they are not a reliable lie detector.

---

People use eye contact, gaze and pupil changes for many reasons, including attention,
light, emotion, habit and culture. The same behavior can have different meanings.
For example, looking down may show respect in one situation, while avoiding eye
contact may reflect discomfort, concentration or a personal preference in another.

Do not use looking away, pupil size or the direction of eye movement to decide that
someone is lying or recalling a particular kind of memory. A three-experiment study
did not support the NLP claim that looking in a certain direction reliably detects
deception (Wiseman et al., 2012). This does not mean that every non-verbal signal is
meaningless; it means that a single cue needs context and cannot prove intent.

A safer approach is to describe what you observed, ask a respectful clarification
question and keep more than one explanation open. Observe people; do not pretend to
read their minds.

---

## Câu hỏi

1. Why can the same eye behavior have different meanings?
2. What does the Wiseman et al. study say about eye direction and deception?
3. What is a safer response than deciding what a person intends?

---

**ĐÁP ÁN:**
1. Meaning depends on context, culture, light, habit and the person.
2. It did not support the claim that eye direction reliably detects lies.
3. Describe the observation, ask for clarification and keep alternative explanations open.
