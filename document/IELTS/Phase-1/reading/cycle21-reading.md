## Bài 1 — The Liar's Body (120 từ)

Forget everything movies taught you about lying. Liars don't necessarily look away, and they don't always touch their noses.

Research does not support a simple body-language formula for detecting deception.
Clusters, changes from a personal baseline and story order may be prompts to ask a
clear question, but they are not proof. Stress, memory limits, culture and the
interview situation can produce the same behavior in an honest person.

The safe rule is: describe the behavior, avoid a guilt label and seek corroborating
information through fair questions. The eyes or body cannot replace evidence.

**Câu hỏi:**
1. What can a behavior cue do, and what can it not prove?
2. Why can the same behavior appear in an honest person?
3. What is a safer next step than labeling someone guilty?

<details><summary>ĐÁP ÁN (nhấn để mở)</summary>

1. It can prompt a respectful question; it cannot prove deception or guilt.
2. Stress, memory, culture and the situation can cause similar behavior.
3. Describe the observation and seek corroborating information without a guilt label.

</details>

## Bài 2 — Congruence (110 từ)

There is a word psychologists love: congruence. It means the parts agree.

When your words, your voice, your face, and your body all tell the same story, you are congruent. "I'm happy for you" with a warm voice and genuine smile — congruent. People trust congruence, even when the news is bad.

When the parts disagree, you are incongruent. "I'm not angry" through tight lips and a tense jaw — incongruent. People feel it immediately, even if they can't name it. Their gut says: something is wrong here.

You already read congruence every day — you just never had the word for it.

The lesson is double: read congruence in others, and build it in yourself. Say what you mean, and let your body say it too. A calm 'no' is stronger than a fake 'yes.'

**Câu hỏi:**
1. What does congruence mean?
2. What do people feel when the parts disagree?
3. What is the double lesson?

<details><summary>ĐÁP ÁN (nhấn để mở)</summary>

1. The parts agree — words, voice, face, body tell the same story.
2. That something is wrong (their gut alerts them), even without naming it.
3. Read congruence in others AND build it in yourself (say what you mean).

</details>

## Bài 3 — Trust Your Gut (120 từ)

You walk into a room and instantly dislike someone. No reason — you just feel it. Then you feel guilty: "I'm judging without evidence."

Don't be too quick to feel guilty. Your intuition is not magic; it is pattern recognition. Somewhere in your past, you met this type of smile, this tone, this posture — and it ended badly. Your brain saved the pattern. It is warning you, fast, before your slow logic wakes up.

But intuition is a smoke alarm, not a judge. A smoke alarm says: "Pay attention!" It doesn't say: "The house is burning." Sometimes the smoke is just toast.

So the wise formula is: feel first, verify second. Trust the signal, then check it with evidence. Ignore it and you walk into bad situations. Obey it blindly and you hurt innocent people.

Feel first. Verify second.

**Câu hỏi:**
1. What is intuition, according to the text?
2. What is a smoke alarm's job (and NOT its job)?
3. What is the wise formula?

<details><summary>ĐÁP ÁN (nhấn để mở)</summary>

1. Pattern recognition — your brain saved patterns from past experience and warns you fast.
2. It says "pay attention" — NOT "the house is burning" (it's not a judge).
3. Feel first, verify second.

</details>
