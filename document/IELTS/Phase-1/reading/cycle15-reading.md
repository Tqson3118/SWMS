## Bài 1 — You Can Change (110 từ)

For many years, scientists believed the adult brain couldn't change. They were wrong.

The brain is plastic — it can rewire itself. London taxi drivers grew bigger memory centers after years of navigation. Stroke patients learned to walk again. Sixty-year-olds learned new languages.

What does this mean for you? It means: you can change your habits. You can learn that skill. You can become calmer, braver, more disciplined — even more intelligent.

The only thing you truly can't change is the past. Everything else has a door.

So when your mind whispers "I can't," answer like a scientist: "Not yet, maybe. Where's the door?"

Your brain is listening to every word you say about yourself. Say hopeful words — it will build hopeful paths.

**Câu hỏi:**
1. What could London taxi drivers' brains do?
2. What is the only thing you truly can't change?
3. What should you say to yourself instead of "I can't"?

<details><summary>ĐÁP ÁN (nhấn để mở)</summary>

1. Grow bigger memory centers (rewire themselves).
2. The past.
3. "Not yet, maybe. Where's the door?" — hopeful words.

</details>

## Bài 2 — The Girl Who Couldn't Speak (120 từ)

At seven, Minh couldn't say one full English sentence. Her classmates laughed. Her teacher said: "Some people just can't learn languages." Minh believed her.

At 22, Minh got a job at a hotel. The manager was kind but firm: "Guests speak English. You can avoid it — or you can learn. Your choice."

Minh chose to learn. It was ugly at first. She couldn't hear the words. She couldn't remember the grammar. But she could smile, point, and try again.

After two years, she was the best English speaker on her team. A guest asked: "How did you get so fluent?" She smiled: "A teacher told me I couldn't. I decided she couldn't be right."

**Câu hỏi:**
1. What couldn't Minh do at seven?
2. What did the manager offer her?
3. What was the moment of change — when did Minh really learn?

<details><summary>ĐÁP ÁN (nhấn để mở)</summary>

1. Say one full English sentence.
2. A choice: avoid English or learn it.
3. She decided the teacher couldn't be right (chose to try, kept failing and trying — accept reasonable answers about persistence).

</details>

## Bài 3 — Superpowers You Already Have (110 từ)

You have superpowers. Seriously.

You can learn anything humans have ever taught — every skill in every book is available to you. Your brain can hold a lifetime of knowledge and still have space left.

You can change your mood with your body: stand straight, breathe slowly, smile — the chemistry follows.

You can choose your response. Between what happens to you and what you do, there is a gap. In that gap lives all your freedom.

You can begin again. Every morning offers a clean page. No debt from yesterday, no interest on yesterday's mistakes.

Most people spend their lives asking for more powers. Fewer people use the ones they already have. Which superpower will you use today?

**Câu hỏi:**
1. Name the four superpowers in the text.
2. What lives 'in the gap'?
3. Which superpower will you use today? (câu cá nhân)

<details><summary>ĐÁP ÁN (nhấn để mở)</summary>

1. Learning anything; changing mood with the body; choosing your response; beginning again.
2. All your freedom.
3. Câu cá nhân — trình bày lý do bằng tiếng Anh.

</details>
