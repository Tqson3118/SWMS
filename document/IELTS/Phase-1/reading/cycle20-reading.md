## Bài 1 — The Interview (110 từ)

Tomorrow is the big day. Linh is going to have her first job interview at 9 a.m.

She has prepared everything. She is going to wear her blue dress — the one that makes her feel confident. She has practiced her answers with her sister, and tonight she is going to review them one last time.

Her friends are worried. "Look at her — she's so tense," they say. "She's not going to sleep, and she's going to arrive exhausted."

But they are wrong. Linh knows a secret: her nervousness is not weakness — it is energy. "I'm going to turn this fear into focus," she says. "And after the interview, I'm going to eat the biggest bowl of phở in Hanoi. Win or lose."

That attitude? She's going to be fine.

**Câu hỏi:**
1. What is going to happen tomorrow at 9 a.m.?
2. What are her friends worried about?
3. How is Linh going to treat her fear?

<details><summary>ĐÁP ÁN (nhấn để mở)</summary>

1. Her first job interview.
2. That she won't sleep and will arrive exhausted.
3. She's going to turn fear into focus (and reward herself with phở either way).

</details>

## Bài 2 — Look at the Clouds (120 từ)

Body language is often compared to weather forecasting.

Look at the sky: dark clouds are gathering. It is probably going to rain — the visual evidence is right there. Interpreting non-verbal cues works in a similar, probabilistic way.

Look at the meeting: Nam is clenching his pen and his jaw looks tight. Is he going to explode? Maybe — or he might simply be focusing deeply or feeling tired. Look at the café: An's eyes keep darting to the door. She is likely going to leave soon. Look at the classroom: the teacher is smiling warmly. She is probably going to share encouraging news.

In psychology, body language is not mind-reading magic. Experts like Joe Navarro emphasize that gestures must always be interpreted against a person's baseline behavior, cultural norms, and the specific context. Signs give us clues, but never absolute certainty.

Practice today: observe three people around you. What do their non-verbal cues suggest? What reasonable prediction can you make?

**Câu hỏi:**
1. What is body language compared to?
2. What signs suggest someone might be feeling tense or focused?
3. Why is body language not considered mind-reading magic?

<details><summary>ĐÁP ÁN (nhấn để mở)</summary>

1. Weather forecasting (probabilistic predictions based on signs).
2. Clenching a pen, tight jaw, or shifting gaze.
3. Because gestures must be interpreted against baseline habits, cultural norms, and situational context — they are clues, not absolute certainty.

</details>

## Bài 3 — The Seven-Second Rule (120 từ)

In interview coaching and popular psychology, people often talk about the "seven-second rule": the idea that strangers form a rapid first impression before you say anything meaningful.

While seven seconds is an informal rule of thumb rather than a rigid biological law, scientific studies (such as Willis & Todorov, 2006) confirm that humans make rapid character judgments within fractions of a second. In those opening moments, people notice posture, eye contact, facial expressions, and clothing.

Is it completely fair? No. Is it a real human tendency? Yes.

The constructive takeaway is intentional presence: stand balanced and upright; offer calm, natural eye contact; give a genuine smile; and avoid rushed movements that project anxiety. But remember: first impressions are only initial hypotheses. Real rapport is built through ongoing words, empathy, and consistent actions over time.

You are going to meet someone important this month — a teacher, an interviewer, or a collaborator. Prepare your calm presence beforehand.

**Câu hỏi:**
1. Is the "seven-second rule" a rigid scientific law or an informal rule of thumb?
2. What initial cues do people rapidly notice in first meetings?
3. How is real rapport truly built over time?

<details><summary>ĐÁP ÁN (nhấn để mở)</summary>

1. It is an informal rule of thumb in coaching, though rapid impressions form in fractions of a second.
2. Posture, eye contact, facial expressions, and clothing.
3. Through ongoing conversation, genuine empathy, and consistent actions over time.

</details>
