# 📖 Đọc — Chu Kỳ 9 (Day 9-11): động từ bất quy tắc

> Ba câu chuyện tình yêu — mật độ động từ bất quy tắc cao, đọc để NẠP bảng động từ vào đầu.

## Bài 1 — Grandma's Love Story (110 từ)

My grandmother met my grandfather in 1962. She was nineteen; he was twenty-two.

He saw her at the market. She bought vegetables; he sold fish. He gave her an extra fish and said nothing. The next week, she came back and brought him a mango.

They didn't have phones. He wrote letters — two hundred letters in three years. She kept every one of them.

They got married in 1965. They were married for fifty-two years.

"Was it always happy?" I asked her. "No," she said. "But we made a promise, and we kept it. That was all."

**Câu hỏi:**
1. How did they meet? (nghề nghiệp của mỗi người)
2. What did he do the next week — wait, what did SHE do?
3. How long were they married?

<details><summary>ĐÁP ÁN (nhấn để mở)</summary>

1. She bought vegetables at the market; he sold fish.
2. She brought him a mango.
3. Fifty-two years.

</details>

## Bài 2 — The Letter I Never Sent (120 từ)

Ten years ago, I knew I was losing my best friend. We fell out slowly — no big fight, just distance.

I wrote her a letter. I told her: "You changed my life. When my father was sick, you came every day. You brought soup. You sat with me and didn't say anything, and that silence was the kindest thing."

I never sent the letter. I thought it was too late.

Last year, she found me online. We met for coffee. I told her about the letter. She cried. "Why didn't you send it?" she said.

I didn't have a good answer. Now I know: never wait to say kind things.

**Câu hỏi:**
1. Why did the friendship end?
2. What did the friend do when the writer's father was sick?
3. What lesson did the writer learn?

<details><summary>ĐÁP ÁN (nhấn để mở)</summary>

1. No big fight — just distance (they drifted apart slowly).
2. She came every day, brought soup, and sat in silence with the writer.
3. Never wait to say kind things.

</details>

## Bài 3 — Attachment Styles (simplified, 130 từ)

Psychology found that we love in four main ways — called attachment styles.

**Secure** people feel safe in love. They say: "I am okay alone, and I am okay together."

**Anxious** people fear losing love. They think: "Did she really mean that text? Why didn't he call?" They need many promises.

**Avoidant** people fear closeness. When love gets deep, they become distant. "I didn't need anyone," they say — but they do.

**Fearful** people want closeness and fear it at the same time: come here... go away.

Why does this matter? Because knowing your style is the first step to a healthier love. Attachment style is not a prison — it is a starting point.

**Câu hỏi:**
1. What do anxious people fear?
2. What do avoidant people do when love gets deep?
3. Is attachment style a prison, according to the text?

<details><summary>ĐÁP ÁN (nhấn để mở)</summary>

1. Losing love (they fear it).
2. They become distant.
3. No — it is a starting point (can change).

</details>
