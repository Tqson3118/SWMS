## Bài 1 — The Best Advice I Ever Got (110 từ)

The best advice I ever received was free. It came from a taxi driver.

I was 25, anxious about my career. "Everyone is more successful than me," I told him.

He laughed. "You compare your beginning with someone else's middle. That's the quickest way to be unhappy."

Then he said something I'll never forget: "The only useful comparison is this — are you better than yesterday's you? Smaller steps count. The turtle beats the hare, every time."

Now, five years later, I am more patient, more focused, and happier than that anxious young person in the taxi. The driver was right: the only person you should try to beat is yesterday's version of yourself.

**Câu hỏi:**
1. What was the passenger's problem?
2. What is 'the quickest way to be unhappy'?
3. What is the only useful comparison?

<details><summary>ĐÁP ÁN (nhấn để mở)</summary>

1. He was anxious about his career — everyone seemed more successful.
2. Comparing your beginning with someone else's middle.
3. "Are you better than yesterday's you?" — beating yesterday's version of yourself.

</details>

## Bài 2 — Types of Friends (110 từ)

Aristotle, the wisest Greek philosopher, wrote about three types of friendship.

Friendships of pleasure: you have fun together — parties, games, jokes. These friends make life louder. But when the fun stops, the friendship often stops too.

Friendships of usefulness: you help each other — classmates, colleagues, neighbors. These friendships are practical. But when the need ends, the friendship often ends too.

Friendships of the good: you both wish the best for each other's soul. These are the rarest friendships — and the strongest. They survive distance, silence, and time.

"The best kind of friend," Aristotle said, "is the one who makes you a better person." Count your friendships. Which column do most of them belong to? And more importantly — which kind of friend are YOU?

**Câu hỏi:**
1. What are the three types of friendship?
2. Which type is the rarest and strongest?
3. According to Aristotle, what does the best friend do?

<details><summary>ĐÁP ÁN (nhấn để mở)</summary>

1. Pleasure, usefulness, and the good.
2. Friendships of the good.
3. Makes you a better person.

</details>

## Bài 3 — The Discipline Myth (120 từ)

People think disciplined people are born disciplined. Wrong. The most disciplined people just build systems.

My grandfather woke up at 5 a.m. every day for 60 years. People said: "Amazing discipline!" His secret was simpler: his body knew the system. Same time, same tea, same shoes by the bed. No willpower needed — just repetition.

Motivated people wait for feelings. Disciplined people ignore feelings. A writer writes badly on sad days. An athlete trains on tired days. Action comes first; motivation follows.

Here is the most important sentence in this text: You don't need to feel ready to start. Starting is how you get ready.

So don't chase motivation. Build your 5 a.m. — your same tea, your same shoes. The system carries you when the feelings don't.

**Câu hỏi:**
1. What was grandfather's real secret?
2. What do disciplined people do that motivated people don't?
3. "Starting is how you ___" — điền.

<details><summary>ĐÁP ÁN (nhấn để mở)</summary>

1. A system: same time, same tea, same shoes — repetition, not willpower.
2. They ignore feelings and act anyway (motivated people wait for feelings).
3. get ready.

</details>
