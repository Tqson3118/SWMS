# Speaking — Cycle 6: cumulative review, D21–D23

Đây là ba buổi D21–D23 ôn tập tích lũy. Không có grammar hoặc core vocabulary mới. Chọn cấu trúc đã học trong [grammar workshop](../grammar/05-personality-description-workshop.md), [omission and reduced clauses](../grammar/04-omission-reduced-clauses.md), và từ core đã học trong [bài ôn Cycle 6](../vocabulary/06-personality-review.md). File này ôn từ cũ, không thêm bộ core mới; chọn đúng từ đã học trong Cycle 1–5. Mọi nhân vật và bằng chứng đều hư cấu.

## Quy trình đúng 50 phút cho mỗi buổi

1. **Phút 0–8:** đọc prompt, chọn 2–3 chi tiết có nghĩa và lập dàn ý ba gạch đầu dòng.
2. **Phút 8–18:** chuẩn bị đúng **1 phút**, nói **60–90 giây**, rồi nghe bản ghi của chính mình và ghi một chỗ khó hiểu.
3. **Phút 18–28:** chọn đúng **hai lỗi** có thể sửa; ghi câu trước và câu sau.
4. **Phút 28–40:** chuẩn bị lại đúng **1 phút**, nói lại **60–90 giây**, nghe lại và ghi một thay đổi có ích.
5. **Phút 40–50:** tự chấm 10 điểm, điền error log và chọn bước tiếp theo.

**Rubric 10 điểm dùng cho mọi buổi:** prompt và 2–3 chi tiết (0–3: 0 bỏ prompt; 1 có ý nhưng quá ít chi tiết; 2 đủ prompt và hai chi tiết; 3 chi tiết cụ thể nối với lý do); mệnh đề/antecedent và ý nghĩa (0–3: 0 không có hoặc sai nghĩa; 1 có nhưng antecedent/attachment mơ hồ; 2 hầu hết đúng; 3 đúng và giải thích được quan hệ); từ core đã học dùng đúng ngữ cảnh (0–2: 0 không có cách dùng phù hợp; 1 có từ phù hợp nhưng còn lỗi hoặc thiếu ngữ cảnh; 2 dùng chính xác và tự nhiên); dễ hiểu và tự sửa (0–2: 0 không hoàn tất; 1 hoàn tất nhưng chưa sửa rõ; 2 bản thu lại rõ hơn). **7–10:** sang buổi tiếp; **5–6:** sửa hai lỗi và thu lại một lần; **dưới 5:** dùng dàn ý ba câu, luyện câu mẫu, rồi nói lại. Accent không bị trừ điểm; chỉ ghi lỗi làm người nghe khó hiểu.

**Error log:** `Ngày/buổi:` · `Prompt:` · `Lỗi 1 và câu trước:` · `Sửa 1:` · `Lỗi 2 và câu trước:` · `Sửa 2:` · `Thay đổi nghe rõ hơn trong bản thu lại:` · `Điểm/10 và bước tiếp theo:`

Nhân vật và nơi chốn là hư cấu. Có thể thay bằng trải nghiệm cá nhân hoặc giữ hoàn toàn hư cấu; không cần nói thông tin riêng tư. Các bài mẫu là spoken English tự nhiên, không phải bài giảng ngữ pháp.

## D21 — A fictional project handover

**Prompt:** Summarise a fictional project handover. Include 2–3 meaningful details: one person or object identified by a relative clause, one active or passive reduced clause, and a clear next question. Reuse earlier grammar; do not invent a new rule. Speak for 60–90 seconds (120–180 words).

<details><summary>Gợi ý 1 — chọn ý</summary>

chọn một người nhận task và một document; ghi hành động thực tế và điều kiện chuyển giao

</details>
<details><summary>Gợi ý 2 — kiểm tra cấu trúc</summary>

object relative may be omitted only when it is object; reduced active/passive must match the noun role

</details>
<details><summary>Gợi ý 3 — giới hạn diễn giải</summary>

kết thúc bằng a next question and state what this handover cannot prove

</details>

<details><summary>Full model (delayed)</summary>

**Natural spoken model (148 words):**

“For a fictional project handover, I met the coordinator who kept the schedule beside the entrance. I remember the session when the handover began because everyone checked the room number first. The document I received listed the tasks that the next volunteer could check, and the checklist prepared by Minh showed which items still needed a second look. The coordinator, who had worked through a short delay, asked me to confirm one time before changing the plan. I noticed that the volunteer carrying the folder had copied the old room number, so I corrected the line and explained the reason. I would ask whether the room will be available when the next session begins. This handover gives us a practical record, but it does not prove that anyone has a fixed working style. A second handover under a different time limit would give another fictional observation to compare.”

</details>

**Actual clause and meaning check:** “who kept the schedule” identifies coordinator; “The document I received” omits object relative that because I received the document; “checklist prepared by Minh” is passive reduced; “volunteer carrying the folder” is active reduced. “when the handover began” modifies the time/event noun **session** and is a relative clause. “when the next session begins” introduces a time clause inside the embedded question about availability, with no preceding time noun that it modifies. Contrast: “the session when the handover began” identifies a session; “When the session began, everyone checked the room number” gives the time of the checking action.

**Worked before/after correction:** Before: “The checklist preparing by Minh was useful.” After: “The checklist prepared by Minh was useful.” The checklist receives the action. A separate active version is “Minh, who prepared the checklist, explained it.”
## D22 — A fictional choice under pressure

**Prompt:** Describe a fictional team choice made under a time limit. Include 2–3 meaningful details: the choice, the person or object linked to it, and a reason you would investigate next. Reuse defining/non-defining, whose/where/when/why, omission, or reduction accurately. Speak for 60–90 seconds (120–180 words).

<details><summary>Gợi ý 1 — chọn ý</summary>

nêu lựa chọn rồi thêm evidence và time limit; tránh nhãn tuyệt đối

</details>
<details><summary>Gợi ý 2 — kiểm tra cấu trúc</summary>

sau “asked” hãy phân biệt “why she chose…” embedded question với “the reason why…” relative clause

</details>
<details><summary>Gợi ý 3 — giới hạn diễn giải</summary>

dùng one setting and one next question; do not claim the choice explains the person

</details>

<details><summary>Full model (delayed)</summary>

**Natural spoken model (141 words):**

“The fictional team chose the route that avoided the crowded entrance during a ten-minute setup. Ana, whose map had three possible routes, pointed to the side door where the signs were already visible. I asked why she chose it, and she said that the reason why it seemed practical was the short distance to the storage room. The volunteer checking the signs confirmed one arrow, while the route selected by the team kept the public path open. I would ask whether the same route works when the side door is closed. That question matters because a single time limit and one building cannot explain every choice Ana might make. Her map records a careful option in this setting; it does not establish a fixed preference. The team could compare another fictional setup and note the conditions before describing any wider pattern.”

</details>

**Actual clause and meaning check:** “that avoided…” identifies route; “whose map…” links map to Ana; “where the signs…” modifies side door. “why she chose it” is an embedded question; “the reason why it seemed practical” uses why as a relative adverb. “checking the signs” is active reduced; “selected by the team” is passive reduced.

**Worked before/after correction:** Before: “I asked the reason why did Ana choose it.” After: “I asked why Ana chose it.” An embedded question uses statement order, without did. A relative version is “the reason why Ana chose it was the short distance.”
## D23 — A fictional review conversation

**Prompt:** Review a fictional conversation after a workshop. Include 2–3 meaningful details: one defining and one non-defining clause, one clear attachment choice, and one cautious next step. Reuse earlier grammar and core vocabulary only. Speak for 60–90 seconds (120–180 words).

<details><summary>Gợi ý 1 — chọn ý</summary>

chọn one speaker and one note; include a place/time only if it has a real noun antecedent

</details>
<details><summary>Gợi ý 2 — kiểm tra cấu trúc</summary>

check whether each clause identifies or adds information; make any fronted participle share its subject with the main clause

</details>
<details><summary>Gợi ý 3 — giới hạn diễn giải</summary>

separate the observed exchange from any claim about a stable trait or future behaviour

</details>

<details><summary>Full model (delayed)</summary>

**Natural spoken model (142 words):**

“After the fictional workshop, I reviewed a conversation with Mai, who asked the group to explain one example before choosing a label. The question that she repeated concerned a task whose instructions were unclear. We met in a classroom where the final notes stayed on the board, and the group slowed down when the bell rang. Having read the notes, I marked the sentence that needed a clearer subject; I did not say that Mai always notices every problem. The learner carrying the folder added one useful detail, while the example chosen by the group showed why the question mattered. I would ask what happens in another workshop with a different time limit. This conversation provides a small record of actions and conditions. It may guide a next observation, but it cannot prove a permanent personality type or explain every future decision.”

</details>

**Actual clause and meaning check:** “who asked…” adds information about named Mai; “that she repeated” identifies the question; “whose instructions…” links instructions to task; “where…” modifies classroom; “when the bell rang” is a time clause. Having read has I as subject, so it does not dangle. Carrying is active and chosen is passive.

**Worked before/after correction:** Before: “Having read the notes, the sentence needed a clearer subject.” After: “Having read the notes, I marked the sentence that needed a clearer subject.” The revised version supplies the reader of the notes as the subject. The relative clause remains attached to sentence.
