# Speaking — Cycle 5: integrated fictional personality observations

Đây là ba buổi D17–D19. Dùng các cấu trúc đã học trong [personality description workshop](../grammar/05-personality-description-workshop.md) và vocabulary core hiện hành trong [Cycle 5 vocabulary](../vocabulary/05-personality-cycle5.md). Không thêm core vocabulary mới. Mọi trait/state/context là quan sát hư cấu có giới hạn, không phải chẩn đoán hay kết luận khoa học.

## Quy trình đúng 50 phút cho mỗi buổi

1. **Phút 0–8:** đọc prompt, chọn 2–3 chi tiết có nghĩa và lập dàn ý ba gạch đầu dòng.
2. **Phút 8–18:** chuẩn bị đúng **1 phút**, nói **60–90 giây**, rồi nghe bản ghi của chính mình và ghi một chỗ khó hiểu.
3. **Phút 18–28:** chọn đúng **hai lỗi** có thể sửa; ghi câu trước và câu sau.
4. **Phút 28–40:** chuẩn bị lại đúng **1 phút**, nói lại **60–90 giây**, nghe lại và ghi một thay đổi có ích.
5. **Phút 40–50:** tự chấm 10 điểm, điền error log và chọn bước tiếp theo.

**Rubric 10 điểm dùng cho mọi buổi:** prompt và 2–3 chi tiết (0–3: 0 bỏ prompt; 1 có ý nhưng quá ít chi tiết; 2 đủ prompt và hai chi tiết; 3 chi tiết cụ thể nối với lý do); mệnh đề/antecedent và ý nghĩa (0–3: 0 không có hoặc sai nghĩa; 1 có nhưng antecedent/attachment mơ hồ; 2 hầu hết đúng; 3 đúng và giải thích được quan hệ); từ core đã học dùng đúng ngữ cảnh (0–2: 0 không có cách dùng phù hợp; 1 có từ phù hợp nhưng còn lỗi hoặc thiếu ngữ cảnh; 2 dùng chính xác và tự nhiên); dễ hiểu và tự sửa (0–2: 0 không hoàn tất; 1 hoàn tất nhưng chưa sửa rõ; 2 bản thu lại rõ hơn). **7–10:** sang buổi tiếp; **5–6:** sửa hai lỗi và thu lại một lần; **dưới 5:** dùng dàn ý ba câu, luyện câu mẫu, rồi nói lại. Accent không bị trừ điểm; chỉ ghi lỗi làm người nghe khó hiểu.

**Error log:** `Ngày/buổi:` · `Prompt:` · `Lỗi 1 và câu trước:` · `Sửa 1:` · `Lỗi 2 và câu trước:` · `Sửa 2:` · `Thay đổi nghe rõ hơn trong bản thu lại:` · `Điểm/10 và bước tiếp theo:`

Nhân vật và nơi chốn là hư cấu. Có thể thay bằng trải nghiệm cá nhân hoặc giữ hoàn toàn hư cấu; không cần nói thông tin riêng tư. Các bài mẫu là spoken English tự nhiên, không phải bài giảng ngữ pháp.

## D17 — A fictional preparation routine

**Prompt:** Describe a fictional discussion leader preparing a meeting. Include 2–3 meaningful details: an observable trait-like tendency, a current state, and a context that may affect the action. Use defining/non-defining clauses and one whose or where clause. Speak for 60–90 seconds (120–180 words).

<details><summary>Gợi ý 1 — chọn ý</summary>

nói một hành động cụ thể thay cho nhãn; thêm trạng thái hôm đó và nơi diễn ra

</details>
<details><summary>Gợi ý 2 — kiểm tra cấu trúc</summary>

“the leader who checks…” can identify one person; “Mai, who…” adds information; whose links a person to a noun and where links a place

</details>
<details><summary>Gợi ý 3 — giới hạn diễn giải</summary>

tách trait-like pattern khỏi state/context: một buổi chuẩn bị không giải thích mọi lựa chọn

</details>

<details><summary>Full model (delayed)</summary>

**Natural spoken model (143 words):**

“Mai is a fictional discussion leader who checks each question before a meeting. I met her in a library where the group could speak quietly, and her notebook, whose pages held short examples, stayed open beside the schedule. She looked tired that afternoon because she had travelled across town, yet she still asked the group to separate an observation from a label. One participant wanted a quick description, but Mai asked what the person actually did and when it happened. I would describe her as careful in this setting, not as someone who is always careful. The library, which had a busy entrance, also affected the conversation because people paused when the door opened. We could repeat the discussion in another place and compare the conditions. That later comparison might refine the fictional account, but it would not prove a permanent personality type.”

</details>

**Actual clause and meaning check:** “who checks each question” identifies the fictional leader; “where the group could speak quietly” modifies library; “whose pages held…” links notebook pages to the notebook. Tired is a state tied to that afternoon, while careful is a cautious description of observed action, not a diagnosis.

**Worked before/after correction:** Before: “Mai, whose checks each question, leads.” After: “Mai, who checks each question, leads.” **Whose** must be followed by a noun, such as “Mai, whose notebook holds examples, leads.” The correction keeps the intended antecedent and meaning.
## D18 — A fictional feedback exchange

**Prompt:** Tell a fictional story about a feedback exchange. Include 2–3 meaningful details: a person whose notebook or task shows a habit, a place or time that shaped the exchange, and a question you would ask next. Use defining and non-defining clauses plus where/when/why. Speak for 60–90 seconds (120–180 words).

<details><summary>Gợi ý 1 — chọn ý</summary>

chọn một notebook hoặc task làm bằng chứng; nói rõ where/when; kết thúc bằng câu hỏi tiếp theo

</details>
<details><summary>Gợi ý 2 — kiểm tra cấu trúc</summary>

whose + noun; where follows a real place noun; when follows a real time/event noun; do not force every clause into one sentence

</details>
<details><summary>Gợi ý 3 — giới hạn diễn giải</summary>

asked why = embedded question asks for a reason; a relative why means “for which” after a noun such as reason

</details>

<details><summary>Full model (delayed)</summary>

**Natural spoken model (155 words):**

“In a fictional feedback exchange, I spoke with Nhi, whose notebook listed two questions for every draft. We met in a small studio where the team displayed its sketches. Nhi chose the draft that showed a clear contrast, but she was quiet when the room became crowded. I asked why she had selected that example, and she said that the reason why it helped was its simple order. Nhi pointed to the first sketch and compared it with the final version. She invited the writer to record one question before changing the layout, so the team could check the choice later. I would ask whether the same choice would appear in a quieter session, because one crowded meeting cannot explain a broad tendency. Nhi’s notebook suggests a habit of asking for evidence, but it does not prove that she will respond in the same way every time. A later fictional meeting could add another observation.”

</details>

**Actual clause and meaning check:** “whose notebook listed…” has Nhi as antecedent; “where the team displayed…” modifies studio; “when the room became crowded” is a time clause. “why she had selected that example” is an embedded question after asked. In “the reason why it helped,” why is a relative adverb linked to reason. These meanings must not be swapped.

**Worked before/after correction:** Before: “I asked the reason why she selected it.” After: “I asked why she selected it.” The first can be grammatical but is less direct here; the second is the embedded question. For a relative clause, use “the reason why it helped” with reason as the antecedent.
## D19 — A fictional role change

**Prompt:** Describe a fictional team member whose role changed during a project. Include 2–3 meaningful details: an observed pattern, a temporary state or constraint, and a place or time that matters. Use a mix of defining/non-defining, whose, where/when, and a reduced clause if useful. Speak for 60–90 seconds (120–180 words).

<details><summary>Gợi ý 1 — chọn ý</summary>

đưa ra hành động và bối cảnh trước khi mô tả pattern; chọn một role change cụ thể

</details>
<details><summary>Gợi ý 2 — kiểm tra cấu trúc</summary>

kiểm tra antecedent của every who/which/whose/where/when; reduced clause phải có chủ thể rõ

</details>
<details><summary>Gợi ý 3 — giới hạn diễn giải</summary>

nói “in this project” hoặc “that afternoon”; không biến vai trò tạm thời thành nhãn cố định

</details>

<details><summary>Full model (delayed)</summary>

**Natural spoken model (146 words):**

“Quang is a fictional team member whose role changed when the project lost one afternoon of rehearsal. The person who usually checked the schedule took the role of presenter, and Quang, who had prepared the visual notes, moved to the timing table. We met in a room where the clock was easy to see, although the room was noisy near the door. Quang looked hesitant at first, but he asked a precise question when the group changed the order. The notes prepared by Quang helped the presenter find the next example, and the team finished the short practice. I would ask how he would work with a full rehearsal before describing any wider tendency. This observation shows a useful response in one context; it does not prove that Quang prefers or dislikes a permanent role. The team could record another session when the time limit changes.”

</details>

**Actual clause and meaning check:** “whose role changed” links role to Quang; “who usually checked…” identifies the person; “where the clock was easy to see” modifies room; “when the project lost…” gives the time/condition. “prepared by Quang” is passive reduced and modifies notes. Hesitant is a temporary state in the fictional session.

**Worked before/after correction:** Before: “Quang, where prepared the notes, changed roles.” After: “Quang, who had prepared the notes, changed roles.” Where requires a place antecedent; a person needs who. The corrected clause matches the actual antecedent and keeps the comma boundaries.
