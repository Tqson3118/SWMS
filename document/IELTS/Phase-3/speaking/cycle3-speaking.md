# Speaking — Cycle 3: whose, where, when, and why

Đây là ba buổi tự học D9–D11, tiếp nối Cycle 2 bằng **whose/where/when/why**. Dùng lại core vocabulary hiện có; không thêm bộ từ mới. Nhân vật và hành vi là hư cấu, hoặc bạn có thể chọn bối cảnh hư cấu hoàn toàn. Không cần chia sẻ đời tư, không có xác minh audio bên ngoài, và không có tuyên bố CEFR hay sẵn sàng cả tháng.

Grammar reference: [relative adverbs and whose](../grammar/03-relative-adverbs-whose.md). Optional vocabulary support: [Cycle 3 vocabulary](../vocabulary/03-personality-cycle3.md).

## Quy trình 50 phút cho mỗi buổi

1. Phút 0–8: đọc prompt, chọn 2–3 chi tiết và lập ba gạch đầu dòng.
2. Phút 8–18: chuẩn bị đúng 1 phút, nói 60–90 giây, rồi nghe lại bản ghi và ghi nhận chỗ khó hiểu trong thời gian còn lại.
3. Phút 18–28: chọn hai lỗi có thể sửa, ghi câu trước và câu sau; đối chiếu gợi ý khi cần.
4. Phút 28–40: chuẩn bị lại đúng 1 phút, nói lại 60–90 giây, nghe lại và ghi một thay đổi hiệu quả.
5. Phút 40–50: tự chấm 10 điểm, điền error log và chọn bước tiếp theo.

**Rubric 10 điểm:** prompt và 2–3 chi tiết (0–3: 0 thiếu, 1 ít chi tiết, 2 đủ hai chi tiết, 3 cụ thể và có quan hệ điều kiện); clause/antecedent (0–3: 0 thiếu/sai, 1 mơ hồ, 2 hầu hết đúng, 3 đúng và giải thích được vai); core vocabulary (0–2: 0 không dùng được từ core đúng nghĩa, 1 có từ đúng nhưng còn lỗi hoặc thiếu ngữ cảnh, 2 dùng đúng nghĩa phù hợp); dễ hiểu và tự sửa (0–2: 0 chưa hoàn tất, 1 hoàn tất nhưng sửa mờ, 2 bản thu lại rõ hơn). **7–10:** chuyển tiếp; **5–6:** sửa hai lỗi và thu lại; **0–4:** dùng dàn ý ba câu và luyện lại. Accent không bị trừ điểm. Chỉ ghi lỗi làm nghĩa khó hiểu.

**Error log:** `Ngày/buổi:` · `Prompt:` · `Lỗi 1/câu trước:` · `Sửa 1:` · `Lỗi 2/câu trước:` · `Sửa 2:` · `Thay đổi trong bản thu lại:` · `Điểm/10 và bước tiếp theo:`

## D9 — A place and a changing routine

**Prompt:** Describe a fictional learner whose routine changes in two places or at two times. Include 2–3 details, one **where**, one **when**, and one question you would ask next. Speak 120–180 words.

<details><summary>Gợi ý 1</summary>

put **where** after a real place noun and **when** after a time noun
</details>
<details><summary>Gợi ý 2</summary>

replace one clause with *in which* or *during which* to check meaning
</details>
<details><summary>Gợi ý 3</summary>

explain why the setting matters without predicting a fixed trait.
</details>

<details><summary>Full model and worked correction</summary>

**Natural model (131 words):**

“I want to describe Linh, a fictional learner who studies in two different places. The library where she usually reads has a quiet corner, and she writes detailed notes there. On the afternoon when the discussion group meets, she moves to a larger table and asks a partner to compare examples with her. The change does not show that Linh is always reserved or always sociable. It shows that the task and the room give her different opportunities. I would ask which part of the routine helps her check an idea before speaking. The group could record another session in which the room is busy and then compare the notes. My account is limited to these observations, so I would describe a pattern of choices rather than a permanent personality type.”

**Clause check:** **where she usually reads** modifies **library**; **when the discussion group meets** modifies **afternoon**. **in which the room is busy** modifies **session**. Both **in which** and **during which** can describe that event; this is not a rule requiring **during which** for every session. The model’s **when** clause has the explicit time antecedent **afternoon**, whereas a stand-alone “When the group meets, …” can introduce an adverbial time clause.

**Worked correction:** Before: “the afternoon where the group meets.” After: “the afternoon when the group meets.” The antecedent is time, so **when** is accurate.
</details>

## D10 — A notebook whose questions changed

**Prompt:** Describe a fictional team member whose notebook changed a discussion. Include 2–3 details, two **whose** clauses (one defining and one non-defining), one **reason why** relative clause, and one embedded question. Speak 120–180 words.

<details><summary>Gợi ý 1</summary>

put a noun directly after **whose**
</details>
<details><summary>Gợi ý 2</summary>

use a defining **whose** clause to identify one person and commas for extra information about a named notebook
</details>
<details><summary>Gợi ý 3</summary>

place relative **why** after **reason**; contrast it with “She asked why …”, an embedded question.
</details>

<details><summary>Full model and worked correction</summary>

**Natural model (138 words):**

“The member whose questions were most specific asked the group to describe evidence before choosing a label. Her notebook, whose first page listed three follow-up questions, stayed open beside the draft. She asked why one comment called a paragraph confusing and invited the writer to identify the exact sentence. The reason why the group changed its discussion was that the questions turned a broad reaction into a task that someone could revise. I would ask whether the same method helps when the team has little time. One meeting cannot prove that the member always asks precise questions or that her notebook causes better decisions. It can show that, in this discussion, the notebook gave the group a useful place to record reasons. The next meeting should keep the questions and record one example that complicates the first impression.”

**Clause check:** **whose questions were most specific** identifies the member; **whose first page…** adds information about named notebook and needs commas. **why the group changed its discussion** modifies **reason** and is a relative clause. **why one comment called…** is an embedded question after **asked**, with no noun antecedent to modify. Both **whose** clauses are followed by a possessed noun.

**Worked correction:** Before: “The notebook, whose was useful, changed the talk.” After: “The notebook, whose first page listed questions, changed the talk.” **Whose** needs a noun after it.
</details>

## D11 — A place, a time, and a hidden question

**Prompt:** Describe a fictional interview in a place and at a time that affected the conversation. Include 2–3 details, one **where**, one **when**, one **reason why** relative clause, and one embedded question. After recording, explain separately why the embedded question is not a relative clause. Speak 120–180 words.

<details><summary>Gợi ý 1</summary>

name the place and time before choosing **where/when**
</details>
<details><summary>Gợi ý 2</summary>

put relative **why** after **reason**, and contrast it with an embedded “asked why …” question
</details>
<details><summary>Gợi ý 3</summary>

mark the embedded question by asking “what information did the interviewer want?” rather than looking for an antecedent noun.
</details>

<details><summary>Full model and worked correction</summary>

**Natural model (146 words):**

“The interview took place in a library where the group could speak without the noise of the main hall. It happened on the evening when the editor had time to read the draft before asking questions. She asked why the writer had removed a sentence, and then asked what evidence the sentence was meant to show. The writer pointed to the original note and compared it with the draft. The reason why the editor suggested a revision was that the sentence sounded certain although the example was limited. The editor suggested a revision and asked when the writer could check the new wording. I would keep a note of the reason, the proposed change, and the time of the next check. This fictional interview shows how a careful question can improve a draft, but it does not prove that one conversation explains the writer’s future decisions.”

**Clause check:** **where the group could speak…** modifies **library**; **when the editor had time…** modifies **evening**. **why the editor suggested a revision** modifies **reason** and is a relative clause. **why the writer had removed…** and **what evidence the sentence was meant to show** are embedded questions after **asked**, with no noun antecedent to modify. **when the writer could check the new wording** is another embedded question; it differs from the time-relative clause attached to **evening**.

**Worked correction:** Before: “the evening where the editor read.” After: “the evening when the editor read.” A time noun takes **when**. Do not replace the embedded question with a relative label.
</details>

## Safety and progress note

Use fictional settings whenever the prompt feels personal, keep recordings private, and choose locally stored files only if that suits you. These sessions practise speaking form and revision; they do not certify CEFR, test readiness, audio quality, course completion, or a full 24-day curriculum.
