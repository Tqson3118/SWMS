# Speaking — Cycle 2: non-defining relative clauses

Đây là ba buổi tự học D5–D7, dùng từ vựng core đã có trong bộ hiện hành; không thêm core vocabulary mới. Nhân vật, nhóm và nơi chốn đều hư cấu. Bạn có thể dùng trải nghiệm cá nhân hoặc hoàn toàn hư cấu, nhưng không cần tiết lộ thông tin riêng tư. Không có xác minh audio bên ngoài, chứng nhận CEFR, hoặc tuyên bố sẵn sàng cả tháng.

Grammar reference: [non-defining clauses](../grammar/02-non-defining-clauses.md). Optional vocabulary support: [Cycle 2 vocabulary](../vocabulary/02-personality-cycle2.md).

## Quy trình 50 phút cho mỗi buổi

1. Phút 0–8: đọc prompt, chọn 2–3 chi tiết và lập ba gạch đầu dòng.
2. Phút 8–18: chuẩn bị đúng 1 phút, nói 60–90 giây, rồi nghe lại bản ghi và ghi nhận chỗ khó hiểu trong thời gian còn lại.
3. Phút 18–28: chọn hai lỗi có thể sửa, ghi câu trước và câu sau; đối chiếu gợi ý khi cần.
4. Phút 28–40: chuẩn bị lại đúng 1 phút, nói lại 60–90 giây, nghe lại và ghi một thay đổi hiệu quả.
5. Phút 40–50: tự chấm 10 điểm, điền error log và chọn bước tiếp theo.

**Rubric 10 điểm cho mọi buổi:** prompt và 2–3 chi tiết có nghĩa (0–3: 0 thiếu prompt, 1 có ý nhưng ít chi tiết, 2 đủ prompt và hai chi tiết, 3 chi tiết cụ thể nối với lý do); relative-clause/antecedent (0–3: 0 sai hoặc không có, 1 có nhưng mơ hồ, 2 hầu hết đúng, 3 đúng và giải thích được antecedent); từ core dùng đúng nghĩa (0–2: 0 không dùng được từ core đúng nghĩa, 1 có từ đúng nhưng còn lỗi hoặc thiếu ngữ cảnh, 2 dùng phù hợp); dễ hiểu và tự sửa (0–2: 0 không hoàn tất, 1 hoàn tất nhưng chưa sửa rõ, 2 bản thu lại rõ hơn). **7–10:** chuyển buổi tiếp; **5–6:** sửa hai lỗi và thu lại một lần; **0–4:** dùng dàn ý ba câu, luyện câu mẫu, rồi nói lại. Accent địa phương không bị trừ điểm; chỉ ghi lỗi làm người nghe khó hiểu.

**Error log dùng mỗi buổi:** `Ngày/buổi:` · `Prompt:` · `Lỗi 1 và câu trước:` · `Sửa 1:` · `Lỗi 2 và câu trước:` · `Sửa 2:` · `Thay đổi nghe rõ hơn trong bản thu lại:` · `Điểm/10 và bước tiếp theo:`

## D5 — A fictional reading-club helper

**Prompt:** Describe a fictional helper who supported a reading-club event. Include 2–3 meaningful details: one action, one condition, and one question you would ask next. Use one defining relative clause and one non-defining relative clause with commas. Aim for 120–180 spoken words.

<details><summary>Gợi ý 1</summary>

chọn một hành động và nơi diễn ra
</details>
<details><summary>Gợi ý 2</summary>

thêm mệnh đề không có dấu phẩy để nhận diện đúng người/vật, rồi mệnh đề có hai dấu phẩy để thêm thông tin
</details>
<details><summary>Gợi ý 3</summary>

nói giới hạn: một buổi không giải thích mọi lần sau.
</details>

<details><summary>Full model and worked correction</summary>

**Natural model (123 words):**

“I want to describe Minh, a fictional helper who prepared a map for a children’s reading event. The map showed the stations that visitors could follow, so families knew where to start. Minh, who had practised the explanation in a quiet library, spoke clearly when the first group arrived. When the room became crowded, he moved the map beside the entrance and asked a volunteer to point to each station. I would ask which part of the route confused visitors, because one successful explanation does not prove that Minh will always speak confidently. The team could record another event and compare the conditions. The question can guide the next observation. My description is therefore a short account of actions, not a permanent label.”

**Clause check:** **who prepared a map** modifies **a fictional helper**, the noun phrase that describes Minh; **that visitors could follow** identifies stations. **who had practised…** is extra information about named Minh and needs commas. “One successful explanation…” is the stated limit.

**Worked correction:** Before: “Minh, that prepared a map, helped.” After: “Minh, who prepared a map, helped.” The correction changes the non-defining relative word and keeps the commas. Another valid answer may use a different fictional helper and equivalent evidence.
</details>

## D6 — A fictional repair-café choice

**Prompt:** Explain how a fictional repair café should assign a task after one busy shift. Include 2–3 details: an observable action, a task condition, and a fair next step. Use one defining relative clause and one non-defining **which** clause. Speak 120–180 words.

<details><summary>Gợi ý 1</summary>

name the task that needs a particular skill
</details>
<details><summary>Gợi ý 2</summary>

add information about a named tool or shift with commas
</details>
<details><summary>Gợi ý 3</summary>

state what the observation cannot prove about the volunteer.
</details>

<details><summary>Full model and worked correction</summary>

**Natural model (129 words):**

“The repair café should give the checklist to the volunteer who checks borrowed tools before closing. During Tuesday’s busy shift, Beth searched for a screwdriver while a visitor waited, but she later made a clear list for two new volunteers. The list, which was placed beside the shared tray, helped everyone see where each tool belonged. I would give Beth a short equipment tour and ask her to test the checklist at the start of a crowded shift. This plan uses the evidence that we have, although it does not prove that Beth is careless or that the checklist caused every later improvement. The café should record the next shift’s conditions and invite Beth to correct the note. A fair assignment describes the task before it describes the person.”

**Clause check:** **who checks borrowed tools** selects the volunteer; **which was placed…** adds information about the named list and takes commas. The last sentence limits interpretation.

**Worked correction:** Before: “The list which was placed beside the tray helped.” After: “The list, which was placed beside the tray, helped.” If the team meant to identify one list among several, the no-comma defining version could also be valid; the speaker must make that meaning clear.
</details>

## D7 — A fictional design-club reflection

**Prompt:** Reflect on a fictional design club that assigned roles using quick descriptions. Give 2–3 meaningful details: an assumption, what a member actually did, and a better question for the next meeting. Use one defining relative and one non-defining relative. Speak 120–180 words.

<details><summary>Gợi ý 1</summary>

contrast the task with the label
</details>
<details><summary>Gợi ý 2</summary>

use commas only for information about an already named person or object
</details>
<details><summary>Gợi ý 3</summary>

finish with a question instead of a permanent category.
</details>

<details><summary>Full model and worked correction</summary>

**Natural model (128 words):**

“The design club assigned Rosa the poster that would appear on its website because the organiser called her independent. Daniel was asked to help everyone, although he already had an examination to prepare for. Rosa accepted two wording checks from other members, and Daniel carried display boards but declined to rewrite a presentation whose subject he did not know well. Rosa, who wanted the poster to communicate one clear message, said that the group should agree on the main decision first. I think the organiser should ask what each task requires, when support is available, and which examples justify a description. The meeting showed that one person can combine a preference with an obligation. It did not prove that Rosa always works alone or that Daniel refuses help.”

**Clause check:** **that would appear on its website** identifies the poster; **whose subject he did not know well** links the presentation to its subject. **who wanted…** adds information about named Rosa. The final two sentences bound the interpretation.

**Worked correction:** Before: “Rosa, that wanted a clear message, explained.” After: “Rosa, who wanted a clear message, explained.” A non-defining clause about a person uses **who**, not **that**.
</details>

## Safety and progress note

Use fictional names and settings whenever a prompt feels personal. Keep each original and revised recording locally according to your own privacy choices. These three sessions are a bounded speaking practice slice; they do not complete a month, certify CEFR, or approve a course package.
