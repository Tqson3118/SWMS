## 🧠 Slot 1 — Grammar (60'): who / which / that

### Mệnh đề quan hệ là gì?

Một mệnh đề phụ bổ nghĩa cho danh từ đứng ngay trước nó — "câu nằm trong câu":

- *The psychologist **who studies traits** believes personality is measurable.*
  → mệnh đề "who studies traits" cho biết psychologist NÀO (chứ nhiều người psychologist mà).

### Phần A — Form: who / which / that

| Danh từ đứng trước | Đại từ quan hệ | Ví dụ (chủ đề tính cách) |
|---|---|---|
| Người | **who** / **that** | The friend **who** listens first is rare. |
| Vật / khái niệm | **which** / **that** | Conscientiousness is the trait **which** best predicts success at work. |
| Người (làm tân ngữ, trang trọng) | **whom** / **who** / **that** | The colleague **whom** I interviewed was very assertive. |

> **Defining = không có dấu phẩy.** Mệnh đề xác định đang nói về AI/CI nào: *People **who are shy** avoid the spotlight.* (chỉ những người nhút nhát — không phải mọi người).

### Bốn tín hiệu thường gặp

| Tín hiệu | Ví dụ |
|---|---|
| Trước là NGƯỜI | She's the coach **who** changed my confidence. |
| Trước là VẬT/KHÁI NIỆM | The test **that** measures openness is free online. |
| Sau "the only / the first / the best" | He's the only person **who** tells me the truth. |
| Tân ngữ chỉ người | The psychologist **whom** the study mentions is Costa. |

### Lỗi kinh điển

- ❌ *The book **who** I read...* → ✅ *The book **that/which** I read...*
- ❌ *The person **who she told me**...* (thiếu tân ngữ) → ✅ *The person **who she told me about**...*
- ❌ Lặp tân ngữ: *The friend **who I met him*** → ✅ *The friend **who I met***

### Bài tập A (làm ra giấy — đáp án cuối file)

**A1. Điền who / which / that / whom:**
1. Ambition is a trait ______ drives people to work late.
2. I admire the therapist ______ wrote that book on self-awareness.
3. The questionnaire ______ we completed took 20 minutes.
4. She is the only friend ______ never judges me.
5. The mentor ______ I trust most is very humble.

**A2. Nối 2 câu thành 1 (dùng who/which/that):**
1. I have a colleague. She is easygoing and witty. → I have a colleague ______.
2. Conscientiousness is a trait. It involves self-discipline. → Conscientiousness is a trait ______.
3. He read a study. The study compared introverts and extroverts. → He read a study ______.

**A3. Sửa lỗi:**
1. The test who measures neuroticism is reliable. → ______
2. The friend who I met her yesterday is stubborn. → ______
3. Resilience is a trait who helps you recover. → ______

**A4. Viết 3 câu về BẠN** dùng who/which/that (1 câu mỗi đại từ), chủ đề tính cách của bạn hoặc người thân.

## 📝 Slot 2 — Từ vựng (60'): 8 từ mô tả người

| Từ /IPA/ | Loại | Nghĩa | Câu ví dụ (dùng relative clause) |
|----------|------|-------|-----------------------------------|
| **easygoing** /ˌiːziˈɡoʊɪŋ/ | adj | thoải mái, dễ chịu | My uncle is someone **who** stays easygoing even in a crisis. |
| **hardworking** /ˌhɑːrdˈwɜːrkɪŋ/ | adj | chăm chỉ | The students **who** are hardworking usually plan their week. |
| **stubborn** /ˈstʌbərn/ | adj | bướng bỉnh | A stubborn person is one **who** refuses to change his mind. |
| **ambitious** /æmˈbɪʃəs/ | adj | có tham vọng | She works with people **who** are ambitious but patient. |
| **reliable** /rɪˈlaɪəbəl/ | adj | đáng tin cậy | A reliable friend is a person **who** keeps small promises. |
| **sensitive** /ˈsensətɪv/ | adj | nhạy cảm | Sensitive people are those **who** notice tiny changes in mood. |
| **witty** /ˈwɪti/ | adj | hóm hỉnh, dí dỏm | The colleague **who** made that joke is very witty. |
| **humble** /ˈhʌmbəl/ | adj | khiêm tốn | Truly smart people are often the ones **who** stay humble. |

### Cách học (60 phút)
1. Nghe từng từ (Forvo.com hoặc AI voice) 2 lần → đọc to từ + câu ví dụ 3 lần.
2. Che cột tiếng Anh → nhìn nghĩa Việt nói lại từ (10 lượt).
3. Viết đoạn **120-150 từ**: "The person **who** understands me best" — dùng tối thiểu **4 từ mới + 2 mệnh đề who/which/that**.
4. Tạo 8 thẻ Anki (Front: nghĩa Việt · Back: từ + IPA + câu ví dụ, tag `P3-M1`).

### Bài tập nhanh (đáp án cuối file)
1. He never panics when plans change — he is so ______.
2. She keeps every promise she makes — completely ______.
3. My brother always defends his opinion — a bit ______.
4. The ______ comedian who opened the show had everyone laughing.

✅ **Tiêu chí đạt Slot 2:** recall nghĩa→Anh ≥6/8 · đoạn 120-150 từ có ≥2 mệnh đề quan hệ.

## 📖 Slot 4 — Đọc (50')

### Bài 1 — Five Letters, One Person (~580 từ — tiểu luận viết riêng cho lộ trình)

<!-- read-start -->
Ask a stranger to describe themselves and you will usually hear a story: the job they do, the city they live in, the family they grew up in. Ask a personality psychologist, and you will get a different answer — five letters. O, C, E, A, N. The Big Five, the model that researchers such as Paul Costa and Robert McCrae built across decades of studies, claims that almost every personality can be described using five broad dimensions: openness, conscientiousness, extraversion, agreeableness, and neuroticism. People who remember the order sometimes use the word OCEAN as a memory trick, and the image is helpful: personality is not a box you are locked in, but a sea you swim in, with currents that pull in different directions.

The first thing to understand is that these five are dimensions, not types. A dimension is a scale that runs between two extremes. Nobody is simply "an extrovert" in the way a coin is simply heads or tails. Instead, each of us sits somewhere on a line — near the talkative end, near the quiet end, or, for most people, somewhere in the middle. A friend who loves parties but needs a silent Sunday afterwards is not confusing the science; she is simply sitting near the middle of the line, where most of us live.

The second thing to understand is what "trait" actually means. A trait is a pattern of behavior that stays fairly stable across time and situations. The child who was curious about everything at the age of five often becomes the adult who asks questions in every meeting. That stability is exactly what makes the Big Five useful: it lets researchers predict, with modest but real accuracy, who will enjoy certain jobs, who will cope better with stress, and who will keep a promise that nobody is checking. Conscientiousness, for example, turns out to be a quiet predictor of success in work and study — the person who starts tasks early and finishes them usually does well, whatever their IQ is.

But stable does not mean simple. Behavior is what a trait looks like when it meets a situation. A shy man who gives a confident wedding speech for his best friend is not lying about his personality; he is expressing what psychologists call a state — a short-term pattern that the situation pulls out of him. Traits are the long rivers; states are the weather above them. If you have ever surprised yourself by being braver, funnier, or angrier than usual, you already understand the difference.

This is also why the Big Five is humbling. Your scores are not a verdict, and they are not a destiny that decides your future for you. They are closer to a weather report about your habits: useful, partly predictable, and never the whole sky. Two people with identical scores can live completely different lives, because the choices that matter — the job you take, the promises you keep, the person you become — are still yours.

So the five letters are a beginning, not an end. They give you a shared language that researchers, teachers, and even employers can use, and they remind you of something easy to forget: the goal was never to fit into a category. The goal is to know your default settings well enough that you can choose when to follow them — and when, like the shy man giving his speech, to step outside them for a moment, on purpose.

<!-- read-end -->

<details><summary>ĐÁP ÁN (nhấn để mở)</summary>

1. Openness, Conscientiousness, Extraversion, Agreeableness, Neuroticism. The sea image is helpful because personality is "not a box you are locked in, but a sea you swim in" — currents pull in different directions.
2. A dimension is a scale between two extremes (people sit somewhere on the line); a type is a fixed category (like heads or tails). Most people are near the middle.
3. A trait is a stable, long-term pattern ("long rivers"); a state is a short-term pattern that a situation pulls out of you ("the weather above them"). The shy man's confident speech is a state — he is not lying about his personality.
4. Because scores are not a verdict or destiny — they are like a weather report; the important choices in life remain yours, and categories are only a shared starting language.
